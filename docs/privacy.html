Privacy policy

Folio for Android and Folio for Windows · Last updated 11 November 2026

The short version

Folio has no servers and no accounts of its own. Your books, notes and reading history stay on your devices. If you turn on Google Drive sync, they are also kept in your own Google Drive. Folio doesn't collect, sell or share anything about you, and it has no ads or analytics.

What stays on your device

Your library, notes, highlights, bookmarks, reading and listening positions, reading statistics and settings are stored in Folio's own storage on your device. Backups you make are saved where you choose.

Google Drive sync (optional)

If you turn on Sync with Google Drive, Folio asks Google for one permission: drive.appdata. This lets Folio keep its own files in a hidden app folder in your Google Drive. Folio cannot see, open or change any other file in your Drive.

Each of your devices saves one small file there. It holds your notes, highlights, bookmarks, your place in each book, which books you've finished, the books' titles and authors, and the device's name. Your books themselves are never uploaded. Folio also reads your Google account's email address, only to show you which account is syncing.

This data goes only between your devices and your Google account. It is never sent to the developer or anyone else. Folio's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

To stop, choose Stop syncing in Folio. That removes the device's file from your Drive and signs out. You can also remove Folio's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. To delete all of Folio's hidden data, open Google Drive on the web and go to Settings › Manage apps › Folio › Delete hidden app data.

Other connections Folio makes

  • Updates: Folio checks github.com/eyuelseyoum97/folio-releases for new versions and downloads them from there. GitHub sees an ordinary download request.
  • Speech models: when you make an exact read-and-listen sync, Folio may download a speech recognition model (from alphacephei.com on Android, or Hugging Face on Windows). Your audio is processed on your device and is never uploaded.
  • Covers and themes: on Windows, when you ask Folio to find a cover (or fill in missing covers), it sends the book’s title and author to Open Library and downloads the cover images it offers. Nothing else about you or your library is sent.
  • Looking up a word: on Android, “Define” hands the word to a dictionary app on your phone or to a web search.

Children

Folio isn't directed at children and doesn't knowingly collect anyone's personal information.
